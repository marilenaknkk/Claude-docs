# Evoto & High-End Retouching: R&D Plan

**Status:** Draft for discussion · **Prepared by:** Marilena & Ronnie · **Date:** 14 Oct 2026
**Pilot clients:** Proper Cloth · L'Agence · Meshki

---

## TL;DR

- **Two workstreams.**
  - **A. Evoto pilot:** test Evoto against the five goals in the brief: automation, productivity, consistency, workflow integration and margin.
  - **B. High-end advertising:** decide which luxury and advertising work we want to target, what sets it apart, and where *editing* stops and *retouching* starts.
- **Start with the tasks, not the tool** (Ronnie's principle).
  1. Break each client's editing guide into small, separate tasks.
  2. Only send Evoto the tasks it can plausibly do.
  3. Measure the **net** time saved: Evoto handling, QC and fix-up time all count.
  4. Spend credits only where they save real time.
- **Start small.** Use about 20–30 images per client (60–90 in total). A recommendation should be ready in **about 5 weeks**.
- **The credit cost is small; wasted runs are what hurt.** At list prices Evoto costs about **$0.05–0.10 per image** on an annual plan. A credit is charged only when a finished image is exported, and editing is free. So it pays for itself if it saves roughly **5–36 seconds** of retoucher time per image (depending on our labour rate; see §4.2). The real threats to margin are:
  - credits spent on images where Evoto removes no work;
  - hidden QC and fix-up time;
  - set-up and preset upkeep.
  
  The pilot is designed to measure exactly these.
- **What we'll deliver:**
  - a go/no-go decision for each task and client;
  - a proposed production workflow;
  - a cost-per-image model;
  - a draft of service tiers that links Evoto savings to a move into high-end retouching.

## 1. Background

### The brief (from management)

Test Evoto for:

1. retouching automation
2. productivity lift
3. consistency improvement
4. fit with our production workflow
5. margin improvement

Use editing guides and images for **Proper Cloth, L'Agence and Meshki**, and start with a small batch. Logins are in LastPass. Jay (Account Executive at Evoto) is on the email thread.

### The 2D team's view (Ronnie)

- Evoto could help the 2D team with **certain repetitive tasks**. Agencies in the Middle East and Asia use it as a **base, not as full automation**.
- **Cost per image** is the key question. Every image will carry an extra cost, and someone has to absorb it.
- **First find the tasks where it really saves time or improves the result.** Don't spend credits on steps that add no value. Build the workflow up from those tasks.
- **Hear from the 2D team** how it could help them day to day.

### Questions this R&D must answer

1. Which tasks in our editing guides can Evoto do to the guide's standard? Or close enough that fixing its result is faster than doing the task by hand?
2. How much time does it save per image, for each client and shot type, once QC and rework are included?
3. Does it make results more consistent, both across a set of images and between retouchers?
4. Does it fit our pipeline? That covers file formats, colour, naming, hand-off to Photoshop, QC, delivery, and our duty to keep client data confidential.
5. What happens to margin, and who pays for the credits?
6. Could the time it frees up help us move into high-end advertising retouching?

## 2. Guiding principles

| # | Principle | What it means in practice |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Tasks first, then the tool** | Map each guide into separate tasks, then decide which ones Evoto should try. |
| 2 | **A starting point, not full automation** | Assume a person finishes every image unless the data proves otherwise. |
| 3 | **Measure net time, not raw speed** | Count Evoto handling + Photoshop finishing + QC + fix-up, and compare with today's process. |
| 4 | **A fair comparison** | Same images, same guide, same QC judges. QC is scored without knowing which method produced the image. |
| 5 | **The 2D team helps design it** | Retouchers shape the workflow; they aren't just test subjects. |
| 6 | **Spend credits deliberately** | Editing and previews are free. Export only inside measured test runs. |
| 7 | **Protect client data first** | Confirm the contract and data terms before any client image goes into Evoto. |

### 3.2 Pilot clients and test images

The image types below are **assumptions based on each brand's public profile. Check them against the editing guides before choosing images.**

| Client | Likely work (to confirm) | What it lets us test |
|---|---|---|
| **Proper Cloth** | Custom menswear: shirts and suits, on-model and product/flat/ghost-mannequin | Removing creases from garments; keeping fabric texture and patterns intact (stripes, checks, twill can warp or show moiré); matching colour to the real fabric |
| **L'Agence** | Premium contemporary womenswear: on-model and product | Skin retouching at a premium standard; texture of denim, knits and silks; consistency across looks |
| **Meshki** | Womenswear, high-volume on-model | Skin and hair cleanup at volume; consistency within a model's set; backdrop cleanup; raw speed |

**Choosing the test images (per client, 20–30 images):**

- Mix the shot types: about 10 on-model, about 10 product/flat/ghost, and 5–10 close-up details.
- Include **hard cases** on purpose: patterned fabrics, sheer or sequinned items, tattoos and permanent marks, flyaway hair against the backdrop, jewellery and hardware, logos and text.
- **Use shoots we've already delivered and the client has approved** wherever possible:
  - the approved final gives us a reference to compare against;
  - logged production times give us a baseline without re-doing the work by hand;
  - published images are less sensitive if anything goes to the cloud.

### 3.3 Test design: three routes

| Route | Description | Why we run it |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Baseline** | Today's manual workflow. Use logged times and approved finals where we have them; otherwise re-time a sample. | The control |
| **B. Evoto + retoucher finish** | Run the client preset as a batch in Evoto, export, then a retoucher finishes to the guide in Photoshop. | **The realistic future workflow.** Gives the net productivity figure. |
| **C. Evoto only** | Evoto output with no human finishing, sent straight to QC. | Shows how far Evoto gets on its own and how it fails, task by task |

**Keeping the comparison fair:**

- Retouchers of similar seniority work routes A and B.
- The same retoucher doesn't do routes A and B on the same image (they'd be faster the second time). If that's unavoidable, leave a gap and shuffle the order.
- The **QC reviewer doesn't know which route** produced an image, and wasn't involved in producing the test images.
- During the test, keep the Evoto app version and the presets fixed. Log both for every image.

### 3.4 Metrics, mapped to the five goals

Targets are **proposals to agree with the sponsor before the test starts**.

| Goal | Metric | How we measure | Proposed target |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Retouching automation** | **Task coverage:** % of guide tasks Evoto does to standard. **First-time pass rate:** % of route C images that pass QC for that task with no human touch. | Task matrix + QC scoring per task | At least 3 tasks where Evoto passes ≥ 80% of the time |
| **Productivity lift** | **Net minutes per image:** route A vs route B, including Evoto handling, QC and fix-up. Throughput (images per hour). | Pilot time log | Net reduction of at least 20–30% on the image types in scope |
| **Consistency** | QC rejection/revision rate. Variation within a set: backdrop values, skin tone, exposure, colour difference (ΔE00) against a reference. Variation between retouchers: two retouchers work on the same images. | QC log; sampler readings on fixed points | Less variation than today, with colour difference within the agreed tolerance |
| **Workflow integration** | Pass/fail checklist (§5.2): formats, bit depth, colour profiles, resolution, naming, metadata, Photoshop hand-off, seats/devices, stability, data compliance | Checklist in Phase 0 and Phase 2 | No blocking issues |
| **Margin** | Net cost per image = labour + credits + set-up and upkeep spread across images. Compared with the baseline. | Cost model (§4) | Positive net saving at current client prices |

### 3.5 Task matrix: starting hypotheses

Ronnie and the 2D team should challenge this list. The pilot will confirm or reject each row. "Evoto tool" is based on Evoto's published features; anything marked *unverified* needs testing or confirmation from Jay.

| Task | Typical guide requirement | Evoto tool | How well we expect it to work | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Skin cleanup (blemishes, temporary marks) | Remove temporary marks; keep permanent ones (moles, tattoos, scars) if the guide says so | AI skin/blemish retouch | **High** | Over-smoothing, plastic-looking skin, removing permanent marks |
| Shine, redness, under-eye | Subtle and natural | AI skin tools | **High–medium** | Flat skin, loss of shape |
| Flyaway hair | Tidy the outline against the backdrop | Stray-hair removal | **Medium** | Halos at the edges, hair that looks cut off |
| Removing garment creases | Remove creases from handling; keep designed drape, pleats and seams | "De-wrinkle clothing" | **Medium** (key test) | Lost fabric texture, warped patterns, designed details removed |
| Lint, dust, loose threads | Clean garment and product | *Unverified* | **Unknown**, needs testing | Removing real fabric details |
| Body or face reshaping | Usually limited or not allowed in e-commerce | Reshape/liquify | **Low, avoid** | Misrepresenting garment fit (more returns), brand policy, retouching-disclosure laws |
| Backdrop cleanup and evenness | Even backdrop to a target value; natural shadow kept or removed as the guide says | Background tools, batch | **High** | Shadows lost, edge artefacts |
| Background removal / cut-out | Clean outline, sometimes with a clipping path | Bulk background remover | **Medium** | Hair, sheer fabric, fine edges; clipping paths needed? |
| Overall exposure, white balance and colour across a set | Consistent from shot to shot | Presets, sync, AI colour match | **High** | AI Colour sends images to the cloud (§5.3) |
| Matching product colour to the physical sample | Exact match to the swatch | Manual | **Low: keep with retouchers** | Colour errors lead to returns |
| Crop, alignment and framing to template | Fixed head/foot lines and margins | Crop and batch tools | **Medium** | Template accuracy |
| Ghost-mannequin / neck-joint compositing | Combining several shots | Not a core Evoto feature | **Low: keep manual** | n/a |
| Detail work: stitching, hardware, logos, text | Perfect product detail | n/a | **Low: keep manual** | AI warping logos or text |
| Resize and export to channel specs | Size, format and naming per channel | Export presets | **Medium** | On Desktop 6.1+ with a paid plan, colour/crop-only exports are free (§4) |

The working version of this matrix is `templates/task-matrix.csv`, pre-filled with rows for each client.

### 3.6 Data capture

Log every image and every route in `templates/pilot-time-log.csv` (or a shared Google Sheet with the same columns). Main fields:

- client and shot type;
- route and retoucher (ID and seniority);
- Evoto app and preset versions;
- minutes spent in Evoto, Photoshop finishing, QC and fix-up;
- credits used;
- whether the image passed QC first time, and its QC score;
- artefacts noticed, and which tasks Evoto handled or had to be redone.

### 3.7 QC rubric (scored blind)

Score each image 1–5 on each criterion. Any score of 1 is an automatic fail.

| Criterion | What "5" looks like |
|---|---|
| Follows the guide | Every requirement in the client's guide is met |
| Skin texture | Clean but natural; pores and texture kept; no plastic look |
| Fabric and material texture | Weave, knit, grain and pattern intact; no smearing or warping |
| Colour accuracy | Matches the reference or swatch within tolerance; consistent across the set |
| Edges and outlines | No halos or cut-off hair; clean cut-outs |
| AI artefacts | No warped patterns, logos, text, hands or jewellery; no texture repeated by cloning |
| Ready for the client? | Yes / No: would we send it as it is? |

## 4. Cost per image

### 4.1 Evoto pricing

Public list prices as of October 2026. **Confirm with Jay; enterprise terms may differ.**

- **How credits work:**
  - You pay **1 credit per unique image you export**. Editing, AI tools and previews are free.
  - Exporting the same image again from the same device is **not** charged again.
  - Importing it from **another device**, or deleting and re-importing it, counts as a **new image**, so it costs another credit. This matters if files move between workstations.
  - On **Desktop 6.1+ with a paid plan**, exports that use only colour, manual and crop tools are **free**. Portrait retouching and AI Colour Match cost 1 credit.
- **Annual subscription:** from $80 for 800 credits up to $1,205 for 24,000 credits, so about **$0.10 down to $0.05 per credit**. Unused credits carry over when you renew (up to 5× the plan's yearly amount).
- **Pay-as-you-go:** from $49 for 200 credits up to $459 for 3,600, so about **$0.25 down to $0.13 per credit**.
- **Enterprise:** custom pricing, which we should ask about at our volume.

### 4.2 The model

```
Net saving per image = (T_baseline − T_assisted) × R  −  C_credit  −  C_overhead

T_assisted = Evoto handling + Photoshop finishing + QC + fix-up   (minutes)
R          = fully-loaded labour cost per minute
C_credit   = actual cost per credit, including wasted exports
C_overhead = set-up, training and preset upkeep, divided across images

Break-even time saved per image = C_credit / R
```

**Break-even: retoucher time Evoto must save per image to pay for one credit**

The labour rates are placeholders. Swap in our real fully-loaded rates.

| Credit price ↓ / Labour cost → | $10/h | $20/h | $35/h |
|---|---|---|---|
| **$0.05** (top annual tier) | 18 s | 9 s | 5 s |
| **$0.10** (entry annual tier) | 36 s | 18 s | 10 s |
| **$0.25** (smallest pay-as-you-go pack) | 90 s | 45 s | 26 s |

**What this means:** Ronnie is right that cost per image matters, but **the credit price itself isn't the main lever**. Any task that saves a minute or more pays for its credit several times over. Margin is lost when:

1. images go through Evoto even though it removes no work (colour-critical product shots, ghost mannequins);
2. AI artefacts add **hidden QC and fix-up time**;
3. careless file handling causes **re-import charges**;
4. building and maintaining presets takes time.

So the workflow needs to **send only the right images and tasks** to Evoto, and keep the rest manual.

**Credits per year** = images *sent through Evoto* per year. That isn't all images: it's only the shot types where Evoto passes the go/no-go test.

### 4.3 Who pays for the credits? Options to discuss

| Option | How it works | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. We absorb it | Built into our cost base | Simple; it pays for itself if the net saving holds | Hides the cost and the value |
| 2. Pass it on to clients | A technology line on invoices | Transparent | Invites haggling; looks like a surcharge |
| 3. Price it into service tiers | An "AI-assisted" tier (faster, lower price) and a hand-retouched premium tier | Clear value for each tier; links to Workstream B | Needs repackaging and client conversations |
| 4. Hybrid | Absorb it on standard e-commerce; high-end work is priced separately | Low friction now; room to grow later | Two models to manage |

**Proposal:** decide once we have pilot data. If the net saving holds, Option 1 or 4 is likely for e-commerce, with Option 3 as the longer-term strategy (see §8).

## 5. Production workflow

### 5.1 Where Evoto could sit

| Option | Flow | When it fits |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Batch first pass** | Ingest → Evoto batch with the client preset → TIFF export → Photoshop finishing → QC → delivery | High-volume on-model sets with repetitive cleanup (likely Meshki, possibly L'Agence) |
| **B. Tool on demand** | Retouchers send specific images or tasks through Evoto only when it helps | Mixed or low-volume work; product-heavy guides (likely Proper Cloth) |
| **C. Sorted at ingest** | Each image is sorted by shot type and complexity: an Evoto route or a manual route | The likely end state, once the go/no-go matrix exists |

In the pilot we test A and B. C is what we'd design from the results.

**Suggested hand-off to Photoshop:** place the Evoto export as a **layer above the original** in Photoshop. Retouchers can then mask Evoto's result back where it went too far (for example to restore texture or a permanent mark). This keeps every change reversible, which matters because Evoto doesn't appear to export layers (see below).

### 5.2 Integration checklist (check in Phase 0 and Phase 2)

| Area | Known so far | To check |
|---|---|---|
| **Input** | Most camera RAW formats, plus TIFF, JPG and PNG. Maximum about 12,000 × 12,000 px and 1 GB per file. | Our capture formats and session structure (e.g. Capture One) |
| **Output** | JPG, TIFF or the original format. **PSD/layers not listed** in Evoto's docs. | 16-bit TIFF? Embedded colour profile kept (Adobe RGB / ProPhoto / sRGB)? Any mask export? |
| **Resolution** | Evoto says it keeps the original resolution | Confirm on our largest files |
| **Presets** | Batch sync and presets supported | One preset per client, with a version number and an owner |
| **Naming and metadata** | n/a | Our naming rules kept? IPTC/XMP metadata kept? |
| **Seats and devices** | Credits are tied to the device an image was imported on | Shared account or team account? Can credits be pooled? What happens when files move between workstations? |
| **Automation** | No Photoshop plugin found in Evoto's public docs | API, hot folder or command line for our production system? Ask Jay |
| **Stability** | Evoto updates may change results | Can we fix the app version? Re-test presets after every update |
| **Credit tracking** | n/a | Usage reports per client or job, so costs can be allocated |
| **Hardware** | n/a | Minimum specs; works offline? |

### 5.3 Client data and confidentiality (must be cleared before using client images)

What Evoto states publicly (Trust Center and privacy policy):

- **Local projects:** only **thumbnails** are sent to the cloud, and they're deleted right after processing.
- **AI Colour and AI Lab** upload full images to Evoto's cloud.
- **Cloud projects** can be deleted at any time and are deleted automatically 30 days after a subscription ends.
- For business customers, **we are the data controller and Evoto is the processor.**

**Before the pilot:**

- [ ] Check each client's contract and NDA for any limits on third-party or AI processing.
- [ ] Ask Jay for a **data processing agreement (DPA)** and a written answer on training: are customer images ever used to train their models, and can we opt out?
- [ ] For the pilot, **use local projects only** and **avoid cloud-processed features** (AI Colour, AI Lab) unless the client has cleared them.
- [ ] Prefer **images the client has already published**. Never use embargoed or unreleased product images.
- [ ] Before any **production** use, agree with each account manager whether and how to tell the client.

## 7. Workstream B: High-end advertising and luxury retouching

**Purpose:** agree where we want to compete at the top end, what it takes to get there, and how the time Workstream A saves can be spent on it. Ronnie is collecting examples, and the call agenda is in §7.5.

### 7.1 Editing vs retouching: working definitions (to refine on the call)

| | **Editing** | **Retouching** |
|---|---|---|
| **Purpose** | Make images correct, consistent and ready for each channel | Make images exceptional; deliver the creative intent |
| **Nature** | Whole-image, rule-based, repeatable | Local, based on judgement and craft |
| **Typical tasks** | Selects, crop/align, exposure/WB, matching colour to the sample, backdrop cleanup, basic skin cleanup, dust and lint, cut-outs, resize and export | Dodge & burn and sculpting with light, frequency separation that keeps texture, refining shape, perfecting garments and fabric, combining several shots, colour grading to a look, beauty work, perfecting products, blending in CGI |
| **Brief** | Editing guide / SOP | Creative direction, art director or photographer, layouts |
| **Time per image** | Minutes | Hours, sometimes days |
| **Volume** | Hundreds to thousands per shoot | A handful to a few dozen per campaign |
| **What success looks like** | Meets the guide, consistent, on time | Changes are invisible, the image is perfect for the brand, and the art director approves it |
| **Pricing** | Per image, volume-based | Per image or per hour, with set review rounds, at a premium |
| **Potential for automation** | **High:** this is where Evoto is strongest | **Low:** at most an assistant for preparation and cleanup |

**The grey zone, "premium e-commerce":** brands that need e-commerce volume with some retouching craft (L'Agence may sit here). This is often where margin is won or lost, and it's a good place to test a tiered offer.

### 7.2 What sets luxury and high-end work apart (to check against Ronnie's examples)

- **Texture is sacred:** pores, fabric weave and leather grain stay intact. Perfect without looking plastic.
- **Light is sculpted:** dodge & burn shapes form in faces, bodies and products.
- **Colour matches the brand everywhere:** accurate across print, outdoor (OOH) and digital, often with CMYK proofing.
- **Detail holds up at large sizes:** billboards and print are inspected at 100% and beyond.
- **Images are built, not just cleaned:** several shots combined, head or limb swaps, extended sets, CGI products.
- **Creative review cycles:** several rounds with art directors, marked-up feedback, brand guidelines.
- **Deliverables:** tidy layered PSDs, masks, and versions for many formats.
- **Restraint:** "less is more", natural bodies, brand ethics. Some markets legally require retouched ads to be labelled (e.g. France since 2017 and Norway since 2022, when body shape is altered).
- **Confidentiality:** embargoes, NDAs, secure file transfer.

### 7.3 Possible target segments (to rank on the call)

| Segment | Typical work | Notes and fit |
|---|---|---|
| Fashion campaigns (seasonal, print and OOH) | Model + garment, combining shots, grading | Natural step up from our fashion e-commerce clients |
| Beauty, cosmetics and fragrance | The highest skin standard, product + skin | Very demanding skin work; strong portfolio needed |
| Jewellery and watches | Perfect products, metal and gem reflections, often mixed with CGI | Highly technical; possible link with the 3D team |
| Leather goods and accessories (bags, shoes, eyewear) | Product retouching, materials, reflections | Close to our current product skills |
| Lookbook / editorial | Lighter touch, creative grading | Lower price, but good for the portfolio |
| **Campaign adaptations** | Extending and resizing master images for every format (OOH, social, digital) | **Volume and premium together**; AI tools to extend images could help here |
| CGI and photo hybrid | Combining 3D renders with photography | Plays to 2D and 3D together; sets us apart |

**How to rank each segment:** budget levels and margin, fit with current skills, size of the skill gap, competition, how we'd reach clients (could existing clients give us campaign work?), and the portfolio we'd need.

## 9. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Client confidentiality or contract breach | Severe | Contract check, DPA, local-only processing, published images in the pilot, client agreement before production use |
| AI artefacts (plastic skin, warped patterns, logos or text, hands) | Rework, quality complaints | QC checks aimed at AI artefacts (§3.7); Evoto output as a maskable layer |
| Garment misrepresentation (reshaping or de-wrinkling changes fit or drape) | Returns, brand complaints | No reshaping of garments unless the guide allows it; check designed drape |
| Colour inaccuracy on product | Returns | Colour-critical steps stay with retouchers and are measured against the swatch |
| Hidden credit spend (re-imports, switching devices, wasted exports) | Margin loss | File-handling SOP; credit tracking per job |
| Vendor risk (price changes, model updates, the company's future) | Results drift, costs rise | Fix app versions; re-test presets after updates; keep the workflow independent of any one tool; benchmark 1–2 alternatives later |
| Team morale or perception | Low adoption | Co-design with the team; position it as removing drudge work; reinvest time in upskilling |
| Retouchers losing core skills | Weaker craft over time | Juniors keep training on manual fundamentals |
| Disclosure laws for retouched ads | Legal and brand risk | Flag body-altering edits; check requirements in each market for advertising work |

## 10. Decision criteria (to agree with the sponsor before Phase 2)

**Go** for a task, client or shot type only if **all** of these hold:

1. Net time per image falls by at least **20%** (including QC and fix-up).
2. The **QC pass rate is at least as good** as today in blind scoring.
3. There are **no unresolved data or contract issues**.
4. The **net saving per image is positive** at current client prices.
5. The 2D team rates usability at **3.5/5 or higher**.

- **Partial go:** use Evoto only for the specific tasks or shot types that pass.
- **No-go:** stop, write up what we learned, and look again in 6 months, or when Evoto releases features relevant to fashion e-commerce.
